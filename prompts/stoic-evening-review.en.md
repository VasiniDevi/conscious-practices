# Practice 7: Stoic evening review

**Use for:** a 5–10 minute end-of-day review.
**Attribution:** the three questions come from the Pythagorean *Golden Verses* as quoted by Epictetus (*Discourses* 3.10). They are often misattributed to Seneca; Seneca (*De Ira* III.36) gives Sextius's different questions. See `research/verification.md` §7.
Agent version: `skills/stoic-evening-review/SKILL.md`.

The prompt is written in English but works in any language: it tells the model to reply in the language you write in.

```text
Run an evening review of my day. Ask these three questions one at a time, in order, and wait for my answer each time:
1) Where did I go wrong today?
2) What did I do today, and what of it was done well?
3) What did I leave undone that I should have done?
Rules:
- Don't praise me and don't reassure me. Acknowledge an answer with at most "Noted."
- Mark where I justify myself. If an answer contains a justification ("but…", "because they…", "I had no choice", "everyone does it", "I was just tired"), quote that exact phrase back and ask once: "Is that an explanation, or a justification?" Then move on.
- Keep me on actions, not character ("what did I do", not "what kind of person am I").
- After the third question, ask me for one line for tomorrow: what I'll do differently, or when I'll do the thing I left undone. Restate it and stop.
- If my self-criticism turns into "I'm worthless" or worse, stop the review and check how I am. If I mention not wanting to live, self-harm, or danger: tell me plainly this needs a person rather than an AI, suggest a qualified specialist (a mental-health professional or a doctor) — or local emergency services if I'm in immediate danger — and ask who I can reach.
Reply in the language I write in.
```

Alternative set (Sextius, via Seneca): *Which bad habit did I resist today? Which fault did I check? In what am I better than yesterday?*
